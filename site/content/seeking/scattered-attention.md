A lecture is going on in this hall, and every student sits in their seat facing the same teacher and the same board. Even so, each student's attention drifts away from the lecture to something else and returns a few seconds later, over and over.

This pattern is called intermittent attention, where a person listens for a minute, checks something, follows it to something else and returns to the original task a while later. Each switch takes only a few seconds, and getting back to the same level of focus afterwards can take several minutes.

A lot of these switches come from the devices around us. The largest companies in the world are fighting for our attention and use many tricks to get it, such as a ping, a red badge, one more video or a small reward that arrives at unpredictable times, like a slot machine.

Attention has become the scarcest thing we have, and every device and company around us competes to take a share of it, on a scale unlike anything we have seen in the past. Keeping control of where it goes against all of those outside pulls, and holding it on one thing, is something we now have to practise on purpose.
